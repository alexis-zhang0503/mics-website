Research Direction 03

Intelligent Communication

Lead: Zijian Lu · Email: 18818732360@163.com

Research Direction

AI-Native Communication from Intelligent Signals to Trustworthy Action

Intelligent communication studies signal understanding, dynamic state modeling, agent decision-making, task-oriented communication, and safe execution under real network constraints. Communication and signal processing are the research objects; AI, agents, and world models are the methods used to turn passive data transport into infrastructure that can sense, predict, coordinate, and act.

Core agenda

AI for Communication uses AI to understand, predict, and autonomously control networks. Communication for AI designs communication support for multi-agent, embodied, and edge intelligence. Both are grounded in traceable evidence, pre-execution verification, recovery, and safe fallback.

Research Background

In 6G, intelligent IoT, the Internet of Vehicles, and low-altitude networks, communication is increasingly coupled with sensing, edge computing, and task control. Networks are expected not only to carry bits, but also to interpret channel and service states and coordinate spectrum, compute, energy, and action resources in dynamic environments.

Conventional approaches often rely on static models and one-shot optimization. Real networks, however, continually change across channels, traffic, topology, devices, and tasks, while observations can be stale, missing, or contradictory. Decisions based on a single snapshot are therefore difficult to sustain through long-term evolution and sudden disturbances.

Network agents can connect service intent, telemetry, analytical tools, and configuration interfaces into a continuous process of diagnosis, planning, tool use, and resource control. Because network operations affect real users and devices, conclusions need provenance, actions need constraint checks before execution, and failures need local repair or safe recovery.

Robots, UAVs, intelligent vehicles, and multi-agent systems further change the optimization objective. Communication must support task completion under latency, bandwidth, energy, and reliability constraints, rather than optimizing throughput alone. The network is thus both an object that AI must understand and control and the infrastructure that enables distributed intelligence and embodied action.

Research Directions

Our signal–network–system agenda begins with observable signals, builds dynamic network state and intelligent decision capabilities, and then jointly optimizes communication, sensing, computing, and action inside real task loops.

01

Intelligent Signal Processing and Multimodal Health

Research targets include wireless channels, WiFi CSI/BFI, millimeter-wave radar, speech, and physiological time series. We study signal enhancement, time-frequency modeling, representation learning, multimodal fusion, cross-device and cross-population generalization, uncertainty estimation, and device-edge collaborative processing.

Speech captured by a microphone is an acoustic signal, not a wireless signal. Speech-based cognitive screening for Alzheimer's disease (AD) is an ongoing intelligent-signal-processing and multimodal-health application, with emphasis on robust generalization across corpora, languages, tasks, and acquisition protocols rather than claims of clinical deployment. Device-edge transmission of audio or task features, federated learning, and privacy protection provide a natural connection to intelligent communication systems.

02

Wireless World Models and Network Agents

We study how channel, traffic, topology, user mobility, service intent, and telemetry can form dynamic network states with explicit provenance and uncertainty. These states support network analysis, planning, tool use, resource control, failure recovery, and prediction of environmental evolution and candidate-action outcomes.

Wireless world models are an active research agenda. They must update continually from incomplete, asynchronous, and bandwidth-limited observations, and compare the performance and risk of candidate actions through counterfactual prediction. Network agents organize models, tools, and control interfaces, verify constraints before execution, and repair plans or invoke safe fallback when conditions change.

03

Embodied and Edge Intelligent Communication

For robots, UAVs, intelligent vehicles, and smart spaces, we study the coordination of communication, sensing, computing, and action. Topics include task-oriented information exchange, multi-agent sharing of state and intent, device-edge-cloud collaborative inference, and safe degradation when connectivity is interrupted or information becomes stale.

This direction develops Communication for AI: a communication policy decides what to transmit, when to transmit it, and where it should be processed according to task value. Latency, bandwidth, energy, reliability, and task success are jointly optimized so that network capabilities directly support embodied systems and distributed intelligence.

Trustworthy Communication-Intelligence Loop

The three research levels are connected by a trustworthy loop of perception, modeling, prediction, coordination, verification and execution, and feedback. This is a unifying agenda for future research, not a claim that one mature end-to-end implementation already exists.

01

Perception

Wireless, acoustic, environmental, device, and task signals are synchronized, enhanced, and quality-checked to produce traceable observations. Sampling rate, link latency, and packet loss determine how timely and complete the agent's view can be.

02

Modeling

Multi-source observations are fused into a dynamic state with timestamps, provenance, uncertainty, and explicit missing or conflicting evidence. Communication budgets constrain what can be aggregated, requiring trade-offs among state fidelity, update rate, and overhead.

03

Prediction

The current state and candidate actions are used to predict possible changes in channels, services, resources, and task outcomes, producing consequence and risk estimates. Prediction must account for stale state caused by network delay and model uncertainty in unseen conditions.

04

Coordination

Agents, edge nodes, and embodied devices exchange the state, intent, and evidence needed for an executable joint plan. Bandwidth and energy constraints require the system to prioritize high-value information instead of indiscriminately aggregating all data.

05

Verification and Execution

Resource, safety, dependency, and service constraints are checked before a tool is called or a configuration is applied, producing a validated action with its supporting evidence. When evidence is insufficient or link conditions change, the system repairs the plan, requests new observations, or enters safe fallback.

06

Feedback

Post-action network measurements, device states, and task outcomes are compared with predictions to update state, models, and policies. The latency and reliability of the feedback path determine how quickly the system can detect deviation and close the action loop.

Future Directions

Future work will investigate world models and digital twins for dynamic wireless environments, verifiable and recoverable network agents, task-oriented communication for multi-agent and embodied systems, device-edge processing of acoustic and wireless multimodal health signals, and low-latency deployment under real device and network constraints.

We aim to evaluate algorithms on real links, real devices, and continuous tasks. Beyond a single prediction or optimization result, the questions are whether the state is trustworthy, the decision is executable, a failure is recoverable, and the communication cost is justified by task value.

Representative Works

A Survey of Blockchain and Artificial Intelligence for 6G Wireless Communications

Authors: Yiping Zuo, Jiajia Guo, Ning Gao, Yongxu Zhu, Shi Jin, Xiao Li

Venue: IEEE Communications Surveys & Tutorials, 25(4), 2494-2528 · Year: 2023

This survey reviews the integration of blockchain and artificial intelligence for 6G wireless communications, covering resource management, network architecture, privacy and security, efficiency, and cost. It provides a broad foundation for AI-native and trustworthy communication systems.
